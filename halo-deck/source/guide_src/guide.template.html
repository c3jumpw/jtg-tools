Using HaloDeck

For everyone

Everything here applies to anyone who opens HaloDeck. There are no accounts or roles. Your decks stay in your own browser until you export or save them.

The getting-started tour

HaloDeck opens with a short tour the first time someone uses it. It walks through the app step by step with screenshots, in two versions: I make decks, and I set HaloDeck up for others for admins. Picking the admin version also tells HaloDeck you run it for others, so you'll see admin changes after updates. The tour is always in Help (the ? button) and in the ⋯ menu on small screens.

{{fig:tour_welcome}} {{fig:tour_step}}
{{fig:tour_showme}}

Show me closes the tour for a moment and rings the real button on screen. Click anywhere, or press any key, to go back to the same step.

The tour's screenshots are built into HaloDeck itself, and they're retaken from the app every time a new version is built, so they always match what's on screen.

What's new after an update

What's new appears once, the first time each person opens a new version. It shows only the changes that apply to them, as cards with pictures, and Show me points at the real control. If nothing in an update applies to someone, they aren't interrupted. Someone who skipped a few updates sees all of them, newest first.

Bringing things in

Three ways, depending on where the picture is:

  • Paste. Copy a screenshot, an image on a website, some text, or a layer in HaloDeck, then press Ctrl+V (⌘+V on a Mac) or click Paste. Pictures arrive as image layers; text arrives as a text box.
  • Drag and drop. Drag one file or many from your computer onto the slide; each picture becomes its own layer. Drop them on the Slides list instead and each picture gets a slide of its own. Drop a PDF anywhere to open it as slides.
  • Open ▾. For a whole PDF, a batch of photos, or a deck saved earlier.
{{fig:menu_open}} {{fig:dialog_paste}}
i

Why the paste box appears. Chrome and Edge let HaloDeck read the clipboard after you allow it once. Firefox and Safari usually don't, and nor does any browser when what you copied is a file. Then the paste box opens: press Ctrl+V there, or on a phone touch and hold it and tap Paste.

PDFs as slides

Open ▾ → PDF… turns every page of a PDF into a slide. It's the quickest way to take an existing PDF to HTML: open it, mark it up if you like, and export as HTML.

{{fig:dialog_pdf}}

Getting the size right

The slide size decides whether a page fills its slide or sits inside it with empty bands. Here is what each choice does to a US Letter page.

Match the PDF
Slides take the page's shapeThe page fills the slide edge to edge. Exporting to PDF gives Letter pages again.
Keep this deck's size
Page fitted inside a 16:9 slideNothing is cut off or stretched; the sides are left blank.
Mixed page sizes
Each page fitted to its slideThe slide size follows the first page you import; other shapes are fitted, never cropped.
  • Each page comes in as a sharp picture, locked so you can draw on it straight away without moving it. Unlock it from Layers if you need to.
  • With Copy each page's text into its slide notes ticked, the PDF's words go into the notes, and from there into HTML exports as real text. Leave it on if the HTML is going to an AI tool or a search: they read text, not pictures of text.
  • Scanned PDFs have no text inside, so they come in as pictures only.
  • Password-protected PDFs can't be opened. Save a copy without the password first.
  • The first time you open a PDF, HaloDeck downloads its PDF reader, so you need to be online for that step.

Photos as slides

Open ▾ → Photos… (or the stacked-pictures button under the slide list) takes as many photos as you pick and makes one slide for each.

{{fig:dialog_photos}}

Adding photos to a deck you already have keeps the deck's size unless you choose Match the photos. That option resizes every slide in the deck, and the dialog says so before you confirm.

Circle, Rectangle and Arrow stay on after each shape, so you can mark up several things in a row. While a tool is on, you can still drag the shape you just drew to nudge it. Press Esc or V, or click Select, when you're done.

Selecting and editing

{{fig:selected}} {{fig:editing}}
  • Hollow circles and boxes only catch clicks on their outline. Click inside one and you reach whatever is underneath, such as the screenshot or a label inside the ring. Shapes with a fill, and small shapes, can be grabbed anywhere.
  • Text hidden under a picture? Double-click where it is. It comes to the front while you type and goes back to its place afterwards.
  • Other ways into text: press Enter with the text selected, use Edit text in the right-click menu, the ✎ button in Layers, or the Text box in the Properties panel.
  • Locked text can still be edited. Lock only stops it moving.
  • Moving precisely: arrow keys nudge by 1 pixel, with Shift by 10. Hold Shift while dragging to move in a straight line. Pink guide lines appear when you line up with the slide's edges or centre; hold Alt to turn that snapping off.

The right-click menu

Right-click on the slide, or touch and hold on a phone or tablet. On a layer you get everything you can do to it; on empty space you get Paste here, which drops what you copied exactly where you clicked.

Layers

{{fig:panel_layers}}

Every picture, shape and text box is a layer. The list shows the top layer first. Click a row to select that layer, which is the easiest way to reach something that's hard to click on the slide.

Hidden layers stay in the deck but are left out of every export.

The exported HTML deck

The HTML file works on its own. Anyone can open it in a browser, with no HaloDeck and no internet needed (fonts fall back to the system's if offline). Pictures are stored inside the file.

{{fig:viewer}}
i

Editing it later. An HTML deck exported from HaloDeck carries an editable copy of itself. Open it with Open ▾ → HaloDeck project… to keep working on it.

Saving and projects

  • Autosave. Every change is saved in this browser as you go, pictures included. The top bar shows Saving… and then Saved. Reopen HaloDeck at the same address in the same browser and the deck is still there.
  • Save project (Ctrl+S) downloads a .halodeck.json file with everything in it. Use it as a backup, to move a deck to another computer or browser, or to hand it to a colleague.
  • Open ▾ → HaloDeck project… (Ctrl+O) opens a project file or an HTML deck exported from HaloDeck. It replaces the deck that's open, so save that first if you need it.
  • HaloDeck holds one deck at a time per browser. To keep several, save each one as a project file.
!

Autosave isn't a backup. Clearing your browsing data, using a private window, or switching browser or computer means the autosaved deck isn't there. Save a project file for anything you'd hate to lose.

Phones and tablets

HaloDeck is built for a desktop browser with a mouse, and works on phones and tablets too.

  • Tap a tool in the bottom bar, then tap or drag on the slide.
  • Tap once to select; tap selected text again to type.
  • Pinch to zoom, drag with one finger on empty space to scroll around.
  • Touch and hold for the right-click menu, including Paste here.

Help inside the app

The ? button in the top bar, Help & guide in the ⋯ menu on small screens, or the ? key opens a quick reference: every tool and its key, where things are, and the main shortcuts. It's part of HaloDeck itself, so it works offline. Its two buttons start the getting-started tour and open this guide; the version you're using is shown at the bottom, next to the change log.

{{fig:help_panel}}
{{fig:help_role}}

Your role, near the bottom of Help, decides which updates What's new shows you. I make decks covers changes to the tools. I run HaloDeck for others adds admin changes: hosting, releases, storage and moves.

There are no accounts, so the choice is stored in your browser. Change it any time.

Troubleshooting

Paste opens a box instead of pasting
Your browser only shares the clipboard when you paste yourself. Press Ctrl+V in the box (touch and hold on a phone). Pressing Ctrl+V on the slide works too.
"That website doesn't allow its image to be copied directly"
Some sites block copying their images into other pages. Save the image to your computer, then drag the file in.
I can't click a shape
Hollow shapes only respond on their outline. Click the line itself, or select the shape in Layers. If it's locked, unlock it there.
Clicking makes a new circle instead of selecting
A drawing tool is still on. Press Esc or V.
I can't reach a text box under a picture
Double-click it, or use the ✎ button next to it in Layers.
The PDF won't open
The first PDF needs an internet connection to fetch the PDF reader. Password-protected PDFs can't be opened; save an unprotected copy. If your workplace blocks some websites, ask your admin (see Network and privacy).
My PDF came in without text in the notes
It's a scanned PDF: its pages are pictures of text. The pages still come in, as pictures.
My deck is gone
Autosave lives in one browser on one computer, at one web address. Check you're in the same browser, not a private window, and at the usual address. Otherwise open your last saved project file.
The top bar says "Not autosaved"
The browser is blocking storage (often a private window or a strict privacy setting). Keep working, and use Save project before you close the tab.

For admins

Whoever hosts HaloDeck

HaloDeck has no server, accounts or admin console. "Admin" here means the person who puts the files online, keeps them updated and helps others when something goes wrong.

Putting it online

Any static web host works. These steps are for GitHub Pages:

  1. Create a repository on GitHub. Make it public, unless your GitHub plan allows Pages on private repositories.
  2. Upload index.html and guide.html to the top level of the repository.
  3. Go to Settings → Pages. Under Build and deployment, set Source to Deploy from a branch, choose main and / (root), and save.
  4. After a minute or two, GitHub shows the site's address. Open it to check.
your-repository/ ├─ index.html ← the app (open the site's address to use it) ├─ guide.html ← this guide; the app's Help panel links to it ├─ changelog.json, CHANGELOG.md ← the change log └─ README.md ← optional
i

Keep the two files side by side. The Help panel looks for guide.html next to index.html. If it isn't there, the panel says so and asks whoever set up HaloDeck to add it. In the same way, this guide shows an Open HaloDeck button only when it finds the app beside it.

Releasing an update

Each new version of HaloDeck comes as a matching pair, index.html and guide.html, stamped with the same build number, plus the updated change log (changelog.json and CHANGELOG.md). Upload all four, replacing the old ones.

{{fig:tour_admin}}
  • Both files are built together from the same source. The build photographs the new app and puts those screenshots into the tour and this guide, so neither can show an older version.
  • What's new appears once for each person the first time they open the new version, showing only the changes that apply to their role (see Change log). Every version has to come with a change log entry before it can be built.
  • If the guide on your site doesn't match the app (say, only index.html was uploaded), the Help panel tells everyone, and names the build numbers, so it gets fixed.
  • The version and build number are at the bottom of the Help panel and at the top of this guide.
  • GitHub Pages republishes within a minute or two; people get the new version the next time they load the page, or after a refresh.
  • Updating doesn't touch anyone's decks. They're stored in each person's browser, not in the files you replace.
  • Decks from earlier HaloDeck versions are converted automatically when they're opened.

Where decks are stored

  • The open deck autosaves in the browser's own storage (an IndexedDB database named halodeck) for that browser, on that computer, at that web address.
  • Nothing is shared between people or devices. Two people using the same link each have their own deck.
  • Clearing browsing data for the site, or using a private window, removes the autosaved deck.
!

Changing the address strands autosaved decks. Renaming the repository, moving to a custom domain or switching hosts gives HaloDeck a new address, and the browser treats it as a different site. Ask everyone to use Save project before you move, then open their files at the new address.

For a planned move, HaloDeck warns people itself. Its SITE_MOVE setting lists the old address and the new one. Anyone who opens HaloDeck at the old address sees this notice, with a Save project button. At the new address, the welcome tells people how to open the file they saved.

{{fig:move_banner}}
  • Publish the version with the notice and give people time to save before you switch the domain.
  • If someone missed it, their deck is still in their browser under the old address. Serving any copy of HaloDeck at the old address again, even briefly, lets them open it and use Save project.

  • HaloDeck doesn't upload, track or log anything. Pictures pasted or opened stay in the browser.
  • Exported files carry their content. An HTML export contains every picture on the chosen slides and an editable copy of those slides, so anyone with the file can open and edit it in HaloDeck. Browsers and limits

AreaWhat to expect
BrowsersCurrent Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari, on desktop and mobile. Opening PDFs needs a browser from roughly 2023 or later.
ClipboardChrome and Edge read the clipboard directly after a one-time permission; Firefox and Safari use the paste box. Ctrl+V works everywhere.
PicturesImages larger than 2400 pixels on their long side are scaled down to 2400 when added, to keep decks fast and files reasonable.
PDF pagesRendered up to 2400 pixels on the long side (1800 for imports over 40 pages).
File sizesPictures are embedded in HTML exports, which adds about a third to their size. A deck of 50 full-page screenshots typically exports to tens of megabytes.
StorageAutosave uses the browser's site storage. Browsers allow a lot, but a full disk or strict settings can stop it; the top bar then shows "Not autosaved".

Rolling it out

  • Publish both files and open the address once yourself.
  • Open a PDF on your work network to confirm the PDF reader can be fetched there.
  • Share two links: the app's address, and the guide (the same address with guide.html on the end).
  • Tell people to keep project files in a shared folder. Autosave is per browser and isn't a backup.
  • Agree on a format for what you hand around. HTML for reading and AI tools, PDF for printing and email, PNG for pasting into other documents.
  • Plan address changes. If the site will move, collect project files first.

Admin troubleshooting

The site shows a 404
Check that Pages is turned on, that the branch and folder are right, and that the file is called exactly index.html at the top level. A first publish can take a few minutes.
An admin doesn't see admin changes in What's new
Their role is set to I make decks. In Help, under Your role, choose I run HaloDeck for others, or open HaloDeck once with ?role=admin at the end of the address. Everything is always in the change log too.
The Help panel says the guide is from a different build
Only one of the two files was updated. Upload the guide.html that came with the same version as your index.html.
The Help panel says the guide isn't on this site
Upload guide.html next to index.html.
People see the old version after an update
Ask them to refresh the page. Browsers sometimes keep the previous copy for a short while.
PDFs open for some people but not others
Their network is blocking cdn.jsdelivr.net and unpkg.com. Allow either one, or have them open PDFs from another network once.
Someone lost a deck
Autosave can't be recovered from another browser or after data was cleared. Their last project file or HTML export can be opened again with Open ▾ → HaloDeck project….
